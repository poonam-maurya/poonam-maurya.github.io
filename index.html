Poonam Maurya

Postdoctoral Researcher at the University of California, Merced

Previously postdoc at Aalborg University, Denmark · PhD, IIT Bhilai

I study how low-power wireless networks behave in the real world, from soil sensors on a farm to transmitters buried beside district-heating pipes, and use measurements and machine learning to design IoT connectivity that keeps working where coverage is hardest.

A LoRa chirp sequence: six preamble up-chirps, then one data symbol per letter of my name.

Research

Experimental wireless communication: radio propagation measurements, channel modelling and data-driven analysis in real indoor and outdoor environments, with a focus on LPWAN IoT for environmental, agricultural and industrial monitoring.

LoRaWAN for smart farming

Designing and deploying a complete end-to-end LoRaWAN architecture, from sensor to gateway to backhaul, at the UC Merced Smart Farm for real-time monitoring of environmental and soil parameters.

F3: Farms, Food and Future

Cellular–satellite multi-connectivity

Machine-learning-driven link activation between cellular and satellite (including Starlink) networks to keep rural monitoring connected, for example during livestock transport, plus CNN-LSTM prediction of mobile network KPIs.

COMMECT · Aalborg University

Sensing beside buried infrastructure

Measurement campaigns and path loss models for 868 MHz links between pipe-monitoring sensors and above-ground receivers, and thermally powered autonomous leak detection for district-heating pipes.

PipeSENSE · Aalborg University

LoRaWAN algorithms and limits

Spreading-factor allocation, distributed learning for tracking performance, relay-mode energy use, improved packet detection for chirp spread spectrum, and a modular hybrid LoRaWAN simulator.

PhD thesis · IIT Bhilai
